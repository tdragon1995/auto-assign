# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Three real audiences share the app, each on their own screens. The **logistics supervisor** works at a desk, monitoring the auto-assign engine, clearing the "Cần xử lý" exception queue, and running audits — a power user in the tool all day. **Office and dispatch staff** create PSC and provincial jobs, add sales/customer entries, and check distances; they are task-focused and not always technical. **Drivers** work on phones in the field, using QR pickup, chấm công check-in/out, and the Nhận Việc self-claim flow — quick, one-handed, often in poor conditions. No single audience is primary; a screen's design is set by whichever of the three it serves.

## Product Purpose

This is the dispatch system for Diag's logistics network: it automatically assigns unassigned delivery jobs to the right driver based on live proximity, per-customer mappings, and shift schedules, and wraps that engine in the surrounding modules a full dispatch day needs. Success means the manual work goes away — the spreadsheet-and-phone-call way of running dispatch is replaced end to end, so people touch the system only for the exceptions that genuinely need a human.

## Positioning

Every screen reinforces the same claim, which holds on four fronts at once: it auto-assigns by real GPS and road-distance proximity combined with shift and mapping rules; it covers the entire dispatch day in one app — assignment, exceptions, provincial PSC/3PL routing, driver self-service, and audits; it keeps automated dispatch legible, so every decision is traceable and overridable; and it encodes Diag's specific 42-location network — the hub and 3PL routing tiers and central-lab flow — rather than being a generic fleet tool bent to fit.

## Brand Personality

Friendly and simple. The interface should feel approachable to a non-technical office worker and to a driver glancing at a phone between stops: plain language, generous touch targets, low cognitive load, nothing that demands study before it can be used. Warmth comes from clarity, not decoration.

## Anti-references

Not over-designed or trendy. No glassmorphism, no heavy or attention-seeking motion, no design-award styling that slows down reading data. Visual fashion that gets between the user and the information is the thing to avoid; when a choice is between looking impressive and being instantly legible, legibility wins.

## Design Principles

Fewer steps, not more screens. Every module earns its place by removing manual work; if a feature adds UI without removing effort, it is working against the point of the product.

Legible automation. The engine acts on its own, so its decisions must stay visible, traceable, and overridable — the user should always be able to see what was assigned and why, and change it.

Meet each user where they are. The supervisor's dense monitoring view, the office worker's focused task flow, and the driver's one-handed mobile screen are different problems; design each for its own context rather than forcing one layout to cover all three.

Restraint over decoration. Reach for the plainest thing that reads instantly. Ornament is a cost paid in reading speed.

Encode Diag's reality. The network's routing tiers, hubs, and central-lab flow are domain truth the product should express plainly, not configuration to hide behind abstractions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Solid defaults: WCAG AA contrast on text and controls, full keyboard navigation, `prefers-reduced-motion` honored on every animation, and layouts that stay readable on a phone in the field. No formal compliance target beyond holding to those defaults consistently.
